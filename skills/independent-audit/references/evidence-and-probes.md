# Evidence and probes

Use for execution/probe evidence, frozen packages, ambiguous absence or claims
of completeness. These checks refine the main method without broadening scope.

## Bind evidence to the actual subject

An authorized checkout needs its actual Git root, relevant HEAD/base and dirty
state. A working-directory argument can still let Git discover an ancestor.
A frozen tree or package instead needs its inventory and file/artifact digests;
do not manufacture Git identity or substitute another checkout. A design artifact
supports specification judgments, not claims about unimplemented runtime behavior.

Tie load-bearing conclusions to the observed source/artifact, command and working
directory, relevant environment/version, exit/result, output and stable receipt.
Source inspection, unit tests, native execution, client discovery, rendered
interaction and external/device behavior establish different layers. Exercise
shipped entry points and examples when those are the claim, not a repaired
substitute. Independently check cited implementer receipts; their provenance alone
neither verifies nor disqualifies them.

## Choose discriminating checks

For a load-bearing check ask both whether it could miss the claimed fault and
whether it could reject a supported valid case. Derive controls from the owner
contract, not from the implementation's expectations. Rejection must have the
required diagnostics and failing exit/result; a green summary is not enough.
Inspect failed setup, collection and probe attempts before interpreting them as
subject failures. Keep corrected attempts distinguishable from the originals.

Preflight tests and examples for shared settings, credentials, live data and
process effects. A disposable checkout does not isolate those. Use only approved
task-owned caches, data, ports and processes; record relevant before/after state
and remove only identified probe residue. Do not manipulate real devices or
change effective permissions to make a test pass. Report unsafe or unavailable
checks and continue independent safe work.

## Reconcile claims with receipts

Quantifiers such as all, none, single source and fully migrated require an
enumerated population. Record sampling, exclusions and inaccessible surfaces.
Search silence, one example or digests for a subset cannot prove completeness.
For every material coverage claim compare the actual decoded inputs, tool call,
exit/output and hashed file set with the submitted statement. Literal backslash-n
is not a newline; an available correct observation does not repair a citation to
the wrong one. Never promote parse success to native operation.

Check production and support paths, suppressed errors, weakened assertions,
exclusions and disabled diagnostics where relevant. Dynamic loading, generated
projections and intentional compatibility can explain apparent absence or
duplication. Corroborate before recommending removal. A historical label or
disclaimer is not immunity when an active route still prescribes superseded work.
Conversely, retained history is not automatically an active instruction or a
distribution defect; inspect purpose, recipients, reachability and effects.

## Keep uncertainty and drift precise

An unavailable runtime receipt means NOT VERIFIED, not that execution failed or
never happened. A missing deliverable explicitly required by the brief is a
different claim and can be refuted by its confirmed absence. Undocumented behavior
may require a policy question rather than a fabricated defect or compatibility ban.

Before final acceptance recheck the relevant bytes and configuration. Refresh
affected checks or identify stale evidence; do not combine an old passing log
with new bytes into an accepted candidate. Final byte equality does not prove
no transient writes or out-of-scope reads occurred. State effective isolation
limits and unobserved effects rather than claiming perfect blindness.
