# Literature review

Use for a substantive synthesis of scholarly studies. Scale the process to an
exploratory brief, focused review or explicitly requested systematic review.
Formatting a bibliography or reading one paper does not require this whole route.

Define the question, populations/settings, methods, outcomes, period and inclusion
or exclusion criteria that matter. Select suitable databases and complementary
discovery routes; no universal minimum database count establishes completeness.
For a formal review preserve its stated protocol and document deviations rather
than silently substituting a quick research stopping rule.

Use reviews to orient terminology and locate primary studies, then follow
backward and forward citations where they can fill a gap. Distinguish title/
abstract screening from full-text assessment. Record actual screened items and
meaningful exclusion reasons when reproducibility is part of the deliverable.
Check query filters against known relevant studies without treating that subset
as all relevant literature.

Treat the study as the evidence unit: preprint, final article and supplement can
describe the same study. Prefer the appropriate verified version while retaining
unique information and material changes. Identify retractions, corrections or
expressions of concern when they affect significant conclusions. An absent
database flag is not proof that no correction exists. State when only an abstract,
preprint or secondary account was accessible.

Extract the design, population/sample, comparator, measurement, uncertainty and
limitations needed for the claim. Separate statistical significance, effect size
and practical relevance. Check applicable bias risks, funding and selective
reporting. A meta-analysis inherits limitations of its constituent evidence;
clinical evidence hierarchies are not universal rankings for software or history.
Journal prestige and author reputation do not substitute for appraisal.

Summarize agreement, heterogeneity, gaps and transfer limits by question. Cite
the source actually examined and distinguish bibliographic verification from
assessment of the study's claims. Use domain reporting guidelines when the work
requires them; a reporting checklist does not prove that the method was followed.
Do not infer a general intervention effect or model ranking from an observational
comparison that changes several factors at once.
