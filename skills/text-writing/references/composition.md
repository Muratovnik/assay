# Composition: turn material into a text

Read for a substantive draft or rewrite, not before every one-line correction.
The examples below are invented teaching examples, not evaluated outputs or
facts about a real author. They show choices, not sentences to imitate.

## Decide what belongs

A useful private summary is: reader, situation, point, evidence, desired result.
Use only the parts that matter. For an explanation, the result can be understanding
rather than a call to action. Select relevant material instead of summarizing every
note equally. An omission is acceptable when outside the purpose, but not when
it removes a qualification, required action or necessary context.

Bad news usually needs the consequence early. A portfolio case needs the problem
and the author's actual contribution. An explanatory article needs a question and
a path to its answer. These are starting choices, not mandatory heading sequences.

## Build the line of thought

Ask what the reader needs before the next point makes sense. Group facts by that
relationship, not by their order of arrival. Put a definition near its first use;
move a digression rather than repeating the main point around it. A transition
can clarify cause, exception or sequence without adding a new fact.

For a long text, inspect the outline as an argument: each section should move the
reader somewhere. Parallel headings are useful for comparable alternatives, not a
reason to invent another section. Keep an ending that resolves the opening question
or leaves a concrete implication. A summary is useful when it aids recall; it is
not forbidden because models also write summaries.

## Supply a bridge, not decorative detail

Suppose the material says a saved search stores filter rules and evaluates them
again when opened. The reader is an analyst who confuses it with an exported file.

> A saved search keeps the filter rules, not a copy of the results. When you open
> it again, the rules run on the data available then. Use an export when you need
> a fixed copy to attach to a report.

The connection makes the distinction usable. It does not claim that exports are
faster, that saved searches never fail, or that a particular product implements it.
A hypothetical scenario should remain recognizable as an illustration. Do not
invent observations to make an explanation feel experienced.

## Write an opening that does work

Invented notes: preview ready; pricing approval outstanding; client must confirm
one approved price by Tuesday noon; preview has no prices; release date unchanged.
A message to a client might be:

> Макет готов к просмотру. Цены пока не добавлены: жду согласованную стоимость.
> Пришлите её, пожалуйста, до полудня вторника. Дата публикации остаётся прежней.

It gives status, gap and request without reciting a production history. Another
wording or a brief greeting can fit the relationship equally well. Nothing here
requires three sentences, a particular salutation or a ban on politeness.

## Make a contribution visible

Invented portfolio material: author mapped approvals and designed a clickable
prototype; the old form revealed required attachments only after submission;
attachments now appear before submission in the prototype; no release data.

> Спроектировала подачу заявки так, чтобы сотрудник видел нужные вложения до
> отправки. Разобрала маршрут согласования и собрала кликабельный прототип для
> проверки сценария. Изменение пока не выпущено, поэтому данных о числе возвратов нет.

The action and design reasoning replace an unsupported claim that the work
"improved efficiency". On a small portfolio card, the release qualification can
be expressed as "Прототип…" instead of reproducing this whole paragraph.

## Revise the thought before the diction

If a paragraph remains vague after word substitutions, return to its point:
who acts, what changes, why the reader should care, and what supports the link.
One developed example can replace several generic assertions. Some background
may still be needed; compression is not automatically improvement.

Then read for natural phrasing. Use a mix of sentence lengths only as the thought
needs it, not to manufacture randomness. Preserve the author's stance and a
worthwhile aside; do not impose a neutral corporate voice on a personal essay.
A requested friendly tone allows courtesy, not fabricated feelings or history.
