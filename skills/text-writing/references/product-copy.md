# Product and marketing copy

Read this only for a genre whose job is to help someone decide: a product page,
a landing section, a feature announcement, a portfolio entry, a conference bio,
an offer. Everything here stays inside those genres.

## Tie a capability to an outcome the user recognises

A feature list tells the reader what the product contains. A decision needs what
changes for them. Name the situation, the thing that happens now, and who it
happens to.

> Weak: "Advanced rule-based routing engine with a flexible policy layer."

> Better: "Route an incoming ticket to the team that owns the product area, so
> the first reply does not wait for a triage round."

The second version is still a claim. It earns its place only if the product
actually does it; a sharpened sentence about a capability that does not exist is
worse than a vague one, because it is more convincing.

## Every claim carries its basis

For each statement ask where it comes from and what would show it is wrong:

- a measurement — say what was measured, over what period, on what workload;
- a guarantee — say who gives it and under what conditions;
- a comparison — name what it is compared against, and do not compare against an
  unnamed "typical" competitor;
- a customer result — it needs the customer's agreement to be used, and the
  wording they agreed to.

When the material has none of these, write the sentence without the claim. Do
not soften an unsupported claim into an insinuation: "helps teams cut triage
time" and "cuts triage time by 40%" both need the same evidence.

## Never import numbers or promises the material lacks

Copy is where invented specifics are most tempting, because they read well: a
percentage, a user count, an uptime figure, a delivery date, a named integration
that does not exist yet, a price. If the supplied material does not contain it,
it does not go in, and no "example figure" is left in a draft for someone to
mistake for a real one. Status words are part of this: something planned,
prototyped or in a private trial is not described as available.

## Keep conversion devices inside this genre

Urgency, escalating enthusiasm, a call to action, a rhetorical question aimed at
the reader's anxiety, a superlative before the evidence — these belong, at most,
to copy the reader knows is selling something. They must not travel into a
neutral genre: a status note, an incident update, a message to a colleague, a
letter answering a complaint, an internal announcement, an explanatory article.
A reader who came for facts and met a pitch trusts the facts less.

Two consequences in practice:

- When a task supplies marketing material as the source for a neutral text, take
  the facts out of it and leave the register behind.
- When the author asks for both — a page and a note about the same release —
  write them separately rather than toning one down into the other.

## An apology is not a campaign

Bad news written as copy reads as evasion. State what happened, what it means
for the reader, what is being done and by when, and stop. Compensation,
gratitude for patience and a promise to do better are only written when the
author has actually decided them.
